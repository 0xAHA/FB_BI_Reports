📖 How this report works

What it shows

Every inventory event recorded against a lot: received, consumed, shipped, adjusted, moved. It is the BI version of the standard Lot History report and reads the same data the same way.

Under each Consume event is the lot the work order made from it, with that lot's own events: where the finished goods went. For the other direction (what went into a lot) use Reverse Lot History.

The trace goes on level by level: when a finished-good lot was itself consumed by another work order, the lot that one made shows under it, stepped in further, and so on. Filters → Levels to trace sets how far (1 is the standard report; the default 5). A lot already listed higher up is named with "events listed above" rather than repeated; a lot that comes round again (a rework loop) is named but not followed.

Recall contacts

The Recall contacts view lists every customer the lot reached, directly or inside its finished goods at every level traced: one row per customer and order contact, with phone, email, the parts and lots they were shipped, the quantity (per part, in its unit), the sales orders and the first and last ship dates. A contact shipped several parts gets a line per part under their row.

  • It always follows the finished goods, to the levels set in Filters, whether or not the finished-good history is switched on for the table.
  • Quantities are net of voided shipments; an order line voided in full is left out.
  • Contact, phone and email are the sales order's; a blank phone or email falls back to the customer's default.
  • If the trace stopped short (the level or size limit), a note says how many lots lie deeper: customers reached only through them are not listed until you raise the levels or press Load all.
  • Click a customer, part or sales order to open it. Excel gives the contacts (a row per contact and part) and the shipments (a row per order, part and lot); Print and PDF print the list.
Searching
  • Type a lot number, or part of one, and press Enter. Every event whose tracking values contain it is listed, so a short search can match several lots.
  • A part number narrows it to that part; % or * is a wildcard.
  • A part number on its own lists every lot event of that part.
  • Over 20,000 matching events, nothing is shown and a note asks for a narrower search; Load all in the note runs it anyway (it can take a while).
  • When the trace stops short, a note says so and how many finished-good lots lie deeper: past the levels you set (Load all traces all ten), or before a level that would take it over 1,000 finished-good lots (Load all loads it anyway).
Reading it
  • Change Qty is in the part's stocking UOM. A part's row carries the net of the events listed.
  • Vendor / Customer is the PO's vendor on a receipt and the SO's contact on a shipment; phone and email are theirs.
  • Yield events are left out: they repeat a movement another event already records.
  • Click a part's row to show or hide its events; Expand / Collapse do every part. Click a part, an event's order, a vendor or a customer to open it in Fishbowl.
  • Click a column heading to sort the events within each part, say by Type or Vendor / Customer (again to reverse, a third time for date order). Each consumption keeps its finished-good lots under it; their events sort the same way. Drag a heading onto another to move the column; drag a heading's right edge to resize it. Your layout is remembered; Settings → Reset column layout undoes it.
  • The Table / Recall contacts / Pivot selector switches to the recall list (above) or a pivot: total Change Qty (or count events) by any two of level (the lot's own events or its finished goods), trace level, part, lot, type, order, vendor / customer and event date. Rows part + lot by type gives each lot's movements and net; rows vendor / customer shows where it went. Click a figure to see the events behind it, with phone and email. Print, PDF and Excel follow the view on screen.
  • The App / PDF buttons in the header switch between this interactive view and the printable pages, laid out like the standard report. In PDF view pick the paper size and orientation in the bar above the pages; they are remembered. Print prints those pages.
