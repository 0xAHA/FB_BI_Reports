📖 How this report works

What it shows

Every inventory adjustment Fishbowl has queued for (or sent to) your accounting system: initial inventory, scrap, cycle counts and cost changes. It is the BI version of the standard Adjustment Report and reads the same data the same way.

Date range is on the adjustment's date (a back-dated scrap counts on the day it was dated).

Signs and totals
  • Scrap shows as a negative quantity and amount: stock and value leaving.
  • Cycle count amounts carry their own sign (a count down is negative).
  • Cost changes revalue stock without moving it; the quantity is the stock revalued.
  • The total is the sum of the signed amounts shown. Quantities aren't totalled: they're in each part's own unit.
Using it
  • Filter changes apply straight away.
  • List is the standard report. By type totals each type; click a type's row to show or hide its adjustments.
  • Click a column heading to sort (again to reverse, a third time for the standard order); in By type the adjustments sort within each type. Drag a heading onto another to move the column; drag a heading's right edge to resize it. Your layout is remembered; Settings → Reset column layout undoes it.
  • Pivot totals Amount (or Qty) by any two of type, part, posted state, UOM, date created and date posted (dates by day, week, month, quarter or year). Click a figure to see the adjustments behind it. Print, PDF and Excel follow the view on screen.
  • The App / PDF buttons in the header switch between this interactive view and the printable pages, laid out like the standard report. In PDF view pick the paper size and orientation in the bar above the pages; they are remembered. Print prints those pages.
  • A very large result shows its first 10,000 adjustments with a Load all button; totals then cover the adjustments shown.
  • Not Posted rows are waiting for the next export to accounting.
  • The Consumption Report shows the same scraps with location, user and customer detail; its totals match this report's Scrap rows, give or take rounding.
