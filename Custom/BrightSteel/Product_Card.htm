📖 How to Use This Report

What a row is

One row per movement, in the order the stock is expected to move. A purchase order line received in two deliveries is two rows on two dates; anything still owed gets one more row on the date it is due. Same on the sales side.

Rows are colour-coded by what they are:

  • Receipt and Shipment have happened — real dates, real batches.
  • On order and Due out have not. They sit on a cream background, and they carry no batch or length because there is no stock behind them yet.
  • Adjust is the report telling you the orders do not explain everything — see below.
Where each column comes from
Invoice NoIN the supplier's invoice, from the PO's Vendor SO #.
OUT our own invoice number, which is the SO number.
Order NoIN our purchase order number.
OUT the customer's PO field — their order number, or whoever rang.
PricePer kilo, in your home currency. The order's own currency is available as a hidden column.
ShipmentThe batch recorded against that movement.
LengthThe batch's average bar length, in metres.
BarsRead out of the sales order line note ("5 bars"). Where the note says nothing, it is worked back out of the kilos and shown in grey as an estimate.
The balance, and the Adjust row

B/F is what was genuinely on hand the day the card opens. Every row then adds or subtracts from it.

A card built from orders cannot see stock adjustments, cycle counts or transfers between sites. Rather than let the closing figure quietly drift away from what is really in the rack, the difference is given its own Adjust row at today's date. If there is no Adjust row, every kilo is accounted for by an order.

The balance beyond today is a projection — shown in grey italics. It is B/F plus everything above it, including what is still on order and still due out.

History rolls first, and closes on real on-hand. The forecast rows then carry on from that closing figure, which is why they are grouped below the history instead of being slotted in by date — an overdue purchase order mixed into last month would otherwise shift every historical balance after it.

Sorting by a different column reorders the rows but each keeps the balance it had on its own date, the way a reprinted ledger does. Sort back by Date to read it as a running total again.

Hover any column heading for what that column holds — several are filled from different fields on a purchase than on a sale.
