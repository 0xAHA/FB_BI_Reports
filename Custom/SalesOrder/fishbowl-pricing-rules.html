Pricing rule guide

How pricing rules work, what precedence means, and how the Tier 2 / Tier 3 module options affect the final sales order price.

1
How pricing rules work — the big picture
Three tiers, two competitions

Pricing rules run through a structured two-stage competition. At each stage, rules of the highest applicable type compete, and the Module Options setting determines whether the winner is the highest or lowest resulting price.

Tier 1
Product Price
The price set on the Product module. No rules compete here — it's the baseline.
Tier 2 — List Price
Rules vs. product price
Tier 2 rules compete. The winning price becomes the list price and flows to Tier 3, stripped of its rule type/rank.
Tier 3 — Sales Price
Rules vs. list price
Tier 3 rules compete against the Tier 2 winner. The final winner is the price on the sales order.
Critical detail: The Tier 2 winner loses its rule type before entering Tier 3. A Customer rule that wins Tier 2 comes down as a plain price — a Customer Group rule can still beat it in Tier 3.
2
Rule type precedence
Which rules compete first, within each tier

Only the rules of the highest-ranked type present at a tier compete. The module option only applies within that group.

①
Customer Rule
Specific customer. Highest precedence — wins unless another Customer rule produces a better price.
②
Customer Group Rule
Customer group (e.g. Wholesale, VIP). Only competes if no Customer rule is active at that tier.
③
Fixed Rule
Fixed-price rule for specific products. Loses to Customer and Customer Group rules.
④
Other Rules
General markups, markdowns. Lowest precedence — catch-all.
If only one rule of the highest type exists at a tier, it wins automatically — the module option has nothing to compare.
3
Module options — what they actually control
Tools menu → Module Options in the Pricing Rule module
Tier 2 → Lowest
Pick the top-ranked Tier 2 rule that produces the cheapest list price.
Use when Tier 2 should pass the best discount downstream.
Tier 2 → Highest
Pick the top-ranked Tier 2 rule that produces the most expensive list price.
Use when Tier 2 sets a cost-based floor before T3 discounts apply.
Tier 3 → Lowest
Pick the top-ranked Tier 3 rule that produces the cheapest sale price.
Use when customers should receive the best available deal.
Tier 3 → Highest
Pick the top-ranked Tier 3 rule that produces the most expensive sale price.
Use when Tier 3 acts as a margin floor rather than an additional discount.
List Price as a base value: When a Tier 3 rule references List Price, it discounts/marks up from the Tier 2 winner — not the original product price. This is how discounts compound across tiers.
